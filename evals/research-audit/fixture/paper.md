# Peer Tutoring and Final Grades in First Year Calculus

## Abstract

We evaluate a peer tutoring program in a first year calculus course with 240 students.
Students who attended tutoring raised their final grades by 15% compared with students who did not attend.
The results show that peer tutoring works for all university students and should be required in every first year course.

## 1. Introduction

First year calculus has high failure rates at many universities.
Peer tutoring pairs struggling students with trained senior students.
Participation in our Peer Tutoring Program (PTP) was entirely voluntary, and students could join or leave at any time.

This paper asks whether attending the PTP improves final grades.
We also examine whether the effect depends on the TSI score.

## 2. Method

### 2.1 Participants

A total of 212 students enrolled in the course in the fall term.
Of these, 38% were first generation students, 45% were continuing generation students and 22% did not answer the question.

### 2.2 Procedure

The mentoring scheme ran weekly one hour sessions from week 3 to week 14.
All students in the program were required to attend at least four sessions.
Tutors recorded attendance.
Students who attended four or more sessions formed the tutoring group, and all other students formed the comparison group.

### 2.3 Analysis

We compared mean final grades between the two groups with a t test.
We also computed the correlation between the number of sessions attended and the final grade.

## 3. Results

The analysis included n = 198 students.
Tutored students averaged 74.2 on the final exam, compared with 66.0 for the comparison group (Table 1).
The difference was significant (p = 0.03).

The number of sessions attended correlated with the final grade (r = 0.41).
This proves that each additional session causes higher grades.

Students with a low TSI score gained the most from the PTS.

Table 1. Final exam grades by group.

| Group | n | Mean grade | SD |
|-------|---|------------|----|
| Tutoring | 84 | 72.4 | 11.2 |
| Comparison | 114 | 66.0 | 12.5 |

## 4. Discussion

Peer tutoring raised grades by a large margin.
Because attendance was voluntary, the program is easy to adopt at other universities.
Universities should make peer tutoring mandatory for all first year students in every subject.

## 5. Conclusion

The PTP improved final grades in first year calculus, and we recommend it for all courses.
