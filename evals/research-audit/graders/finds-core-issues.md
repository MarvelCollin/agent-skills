The fixture is a short Markdown paper on peer tutoring in first year calculus with eleven planted problems:

1. The sample size changes without explanation: 240 students in the abstract, 212 enrolled in Section 2.1, and n = 198 analyzed in Section 3.
2. The abstract claims grades rose by 15%, but Table 1 gives 72.4 against 66.0, a gap of 6.4 points or about 10 percent.
3. The Results text says tutored students averaged 74.2, while Table 1 says 72.4.
4. The participant percentages in Section 2.1 (38%, 45% and 22%) sum to 105.
5. A causal claim from a correlation: "This proves that each additional session causes higher grades" rests on r = 0.41.
6. The groups are self selected by attendance and nothing controls for prior ability or other confounders, so the group difference cannot be read as the program's effect (missing comparison design or adjustment).
7. Overgeneralization: one calculus course at one university is used to claim the program "works for all university students" and should be required "in every first year course" or "every subject".
8. The "TSI score" is never defined, and the claim that low TSI students gained the most has no reported analysis.
9. The program has inconsistent names: Peer Tutoring Program (PTP), "the mentoring scheme" and "PTS".
10. A contradiction: the Introduction and Discussion say participation was voluntary, while Section 2.2 says students were required to attend at least four sessions.
11. There is no limitations or threats to validity section.
